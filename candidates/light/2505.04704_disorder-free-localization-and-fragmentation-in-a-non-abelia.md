# Disorder-Free Localization and Fragmentation in a Non-Abelian Lattice Gauge Theory

**Authors:** G. Cataldi, G. Calajo, P. Silvi, S. Montangero, J. C. Halimeh

**Citation:** preprint, 2026

**arXiv:** [2505.04704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04704)

**DOI:** [10.1103/fhfy-dh85](https://doi.org/10.1103/fhfy-dh85)

## Abstract

We investigate how isolated quantum many-body systems dynamically equilibrate under non-Abelian gauge-symmetry constraints. By encoding gauge superselection sectors into static $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ background charges, we map out the dynamical phase diagram of a (1+1)D $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ lattice gauge theory with dynamical matter. We uncover three distinct regimes: (i) an ergodic phase, (ii) a fragmented phase that is nonthermal but delocalized, and (iii) a disorder-free many-body localized regime. In the latter, a superposition of gauge superselection sectors preserves spatial matter inhomogeneities in time, as evidenced by distinct temporal scalings of entropy. We highlight the non-Abelian nature of these phases and argue for potential realizations on qudit processors.

We investigate how isolated quantum many-body systems dynamically equilibrate under nonAbelian gauge-symmetry constraints. By encoding gauge superselection sectors into static SU(2) background charges, we map out the dynamical phase diagram of a (1+1)D SU(2) lattice gauge theory with dynamical matter. We uncover three distinct regimes: (i) an ergodic phase, (ii) a fragmented phase that is nonthermal but delocalized, and (iii) a disorder-free many-body localized regime. In the latter, a superposition of gauge superselection sectors preserves spatial matter inhomogeneities in time, as evidenced by distinct temporal scalings of entropy. We highlight the non-Abelian nature of these phases and argue for potential realizations on qudit processors. 

_**Introduction.—**_ Lattice gauge theories (LGTs) provide a non-perturbative formulation of continuum gauge theories [1–3], where spacetime is discretized into a grid and gauge fields are represented by link variables [4]. Originally developed to investigate quark (de)confinement in quantum chromodynamics [5–7], LGTs have become central to a surge of quantum simulation experiments [8–48] in the last decade. More recently, they have emerged as powerful tools for studying quantum many-body (QMB) dynamics [49]. In particular, due to the presence of gauge symmetry [50, 51], which enforces strong local constraints on dynamics, LGTs have been successful in discovering and exploring novel types of nonthermal behavior that circumvent the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH) [52–60]. In generic interacting isolated quantum systems, if ETH holds, then for initial pure states with a narrow energy distribution, local observables (or equivalently, sufficiently small subsystems) relax at long times to values predicted by the thermal ensemble fixed by the initial state energy [56, 61]. A paradigmatic ergodicity-breaking phenomenon in LGTs is QMB scarring [62–75], where special eigenstates, often equally spaced in energy, exhibit anomalously low entanglement entropy [76–78]. Scars have been experimentally observed in LGTs [18, 62, 79] and theoretically shown to persist toward the lattice quantum field theory limit [80, 81], in one and higher spatial dimensions [82, 83], for Abelian [84–93], and non-Abelian gauge groups [75]. 

Other examples of nonthermal dynamics in LGTs are Hilbert space fragmentation (HSF) [94, 95], and disorderfree localization (DFL) [96, 97]. _**Summary and outlook.—**_ We have investigated the dynamical phases of a (1+1)D truncated SU(2) LGT with dynamical matter. To explore the dynamics across different gauge superselection sectors, we developed a formalism based on static SU(2) background charges, enabling the preparation of superpositions across distinct sectors. Within this framework, we identified three different regimes: in addition to a thermal ergodic phase, we found a nonthermal yet delocalized fragmented regime and a DFL phase that emerges when superpositions of gauge superselection sectors are considered. In the latter, an initial imbalance in the matter distribution persists over time, and the entanglement entropy grows logarithmically, reminiscent of MBL. The dynamics in these regimes are strongly shaped by non-Abelian excitations such as baryons. In the DFL regime, characterized by large gauge coupling and mass, the gauge-field and background-charge truncations are well controlled. We expect the qualitative 

features of the dynamics to remain robust under larger truncation cutoffs, as supported by recent results on QMB scars in the same model [75]. 

Several immediate directions follow from our work, such as the stability of the DFL phase in (2+1)D and under different non-Abelian gauge groups. Another interesting avenue is to analyze the robustness of DFL to coherent error terms that explicitly break the non-Abelian gauge symmetry, which are commonly encountered in quantum simulators. The effect of decoherence on this phase is also a critical direction worth investigating. 

Finally, we point out that a qudit formulation with a 13-dimensional local (single-site) Hilbert space faithfully captures the DFL phenomenology described in this work. This makes the model particularly suitable for digital quantum simulation on qudit-based quantum processors, as recently proposed for the same model in the absence of background charges [136]. Platforms supporting such large local dimensions are already experimentally accessible, as demonstrated by recent implementations using 13level trapped-ion qudits based on[137] Ba[+] [147], as well as other qudit architectures employing metastable states of ion isotopes [148] and circular Rydberg atoms [149, 150].

## **INITIAL STATE CANDIDATES FOR DYNAMICS** 

To observe DFL, we start the dynamics with an equal superposition of all the possible configurations of singlesite background charges, i.e., a superposition of all the _N_ SS _superselection sectors {b[κ] n[}]_[=][[] _[b][κ]_ 1 _[. . . b][κ] N_[]] _[N] κ_ =1[SS][,][as][in] Eq.(3).
